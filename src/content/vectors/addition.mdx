# The parallelogram

Two pushes act on the same point at once. One eastward, three meters. One northeastward, two meters. What's the net push?

You could try writing it out in words. You'd run out of patience by lunchtime.

The trick — known to Stevin, formalized by Newton, made notational by Gibbs three centuries later — is to lay the second push *starting from the tip of the first*. The total push is the arrow from where you started to where you ended up.

You can do the same thing by drawing parallel copies of both arrows and completing the parallelogram. The diagonal is the sum.

Drag either tip on the left. Watch the parallelogram complete itself. The vermilion arrow is `v + w`.

Now try to drag them so they point the same direction. Make them parallel.

You just collapsed it. The parallelogram folded into a line. Heaviside loved this case; Tait considered it crude. We'll deal with their fight at the end. For now: notice that even when the parallelogram is degenerate, the *sum* still makes sense. It's still a vector along the same line, just with a length equal to the two pushes added up.
