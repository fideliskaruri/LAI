# Location vs. journey

A point describes *where*. But the physics that drove Stevin and Tait and Gibbs needs to describe *journeys*. Three meters east. A push of ten newtons northeast. A velocity of five meters per second downward.

These all have something a point doesn't: direction.

The trick: instead of a single point, use two. Pick a starting point. Pick an ending point. Draw an arrow between them. That arrow is a *vector*.

A vector's two components — what you might call its `(x, y)` — measure the *difference* between its endpoints. Not where the arrow sits in space.

Drag the tip on the left. The components update. Now drag the *shaft* instead — the whole arrow slides somewhere else. Watch the readout.

The components stay the same.

That's the central trick. A vector is the journey, not the location. You can pick it up, move it across the room, set it down — and it's still the same vector. The direction and length are what matter. Where you happen to draw it is just convenience.

We're going to use this freedom in the next scene.
