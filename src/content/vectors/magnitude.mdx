# How long is an arrow?

We've been talking about pushes and journeys. But how big is a push? How long is a journey?

For a vector with components `(x, y)`, the answer is in a picture that Pythagoras drew twenty-five hundred years ago. The arrow is the hypotenuse of a right triangle. The horizontal leg has length `|x|`, the vertical leg `|y|`. By Pythagoras's identity, the hypotenuse has length

$$
|v| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}
$$

Drag the tip on the left. The dashed right triangle redraws itself. The magnitude readout updates.

If you've forgotten *why* `c² = a² + b²` — and there's no shame in that — draw a square of side `a + b`. Drop the same right triangle inside it four times, each rotated, leaving a smaller square of side `c` in the middle. Add up the areas. The pieces have to match. That's the proof. It's been the proof for two and a half thousand years.

For a 3D vector — three components — you just add a third squared term. For a 1000-dimensional vector (and we're going there, several chapters from now) the same idea: sum of squares, take the root. Pythagoras keeps working.
