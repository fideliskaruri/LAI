# The leap

Up to now, every vector you've touched has had two components.

The arrows live in a plane. They have an x, a y. You can draw them. You can measure them. The picture is the math; the math is the picture.

Keep scrolling.

</Act>

<Act id="nd-2" eyebrow="Ten · 3D">

# A third axis

But the world isn't flat. Most pushes you can imagine — a kicked ball, a falling raindrop, a stretched muscle — have *three* components. East. North. Up.

Vectors handle this without complaining. Add a third number to the column. Pythagoras becomes `|v| = √(x² + y² + z²)`. The parallelogram becomes a parallelepiped — a tilted box. The arrow becomes harder to draw on paper but easier to imagine if you've ever held a real object in space.

The 3D scene on the left rotates slowly. Same arrow you've been touching, with one more dimension to live in.

</Act>

<Act id="nd-3" eyebrow="Ten · The picture runs out of room">

# Four. Five. A thousand.

Now: four dimensions.

You can't draw four perpendicular axes. Three is the limit of paper, of stone, of the world you can see. But the math doesn't care. Four components is just four numbers. The dot product is still a sum. Pythagoras still works — sum of squares, take the root. You can compute a distance between two points in four-dimensional space without ever picturing where they sit.

The axes are fading. The arrow is still there. So are the numbers — see them appear, on the right.

</Act>

<Act id="nd-4" eyebrow="Ten · The numbers stay">

# What was always there

And finally — the picture goes.

What's left is the column of numbers. That, in the end, is all a vector ever was. A list of components. The picture was a kindness, a way to help your eyes do what the math was already doing.

In modern language models the vectors have *hundreds* of components. *Thousands.* You can't draw them, you can't picture them, but you can add them, scale them, and measure their length using exactly the same rules Stevin and Hamilton and Gibbs used. The math is the same. There's just more of it.

</Act>
