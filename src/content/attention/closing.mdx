# Stack this. Train on the internet. Wait.

Look at the column on the left.

Six identical-looking boxes, stacked vertically, each labelled *self-attention layer*. Tokens come in at the bottom; predictions come out at the top. Each layer takes the previous layer's vectors and lets them attend to each other again, with its own freshly-learned projection matrices. The drawing tops out at six layers because there isn't room for more. The real numbers are bigger.

- The 2017 *Attention Is All You Need* transformer: **6 layers**, **8 heads** per layer.
- BERT-Base, 2018: **12 layers**, **12 heads**.
- GPT-2 XL, 2019: **48 layers**, **25 heads**.
- GPT-3, 2020: **96 layers**, **96 heads**, with a model dimension of 12,288.
- GPT-4 is rumoured to be deeper still &mdash; the architecture details aren't public, but the shape is the same.

That's what a modern language model *is*. A column of self-attention layers, deep. The mechanism in each layer is what we just built: query, key, value, softmax, weighted sum. The layers are stacked with feed-forward networks and residual connections between them, all the way up. The whole thing is trained end-to-end by gradient descent &mdash; the chapter you'll find at /optimization &mdash; on the loss of predicting the next token in a giant pile of text.

The whole pile of text matters. GPT-3 was trained on roughly 500 billion tokens of internet text, books, and code. GPT-4's training set isn't disclosed but is in the trillions. When you push *this much* attention through *this much* data, the model isn't just learning the subject-verb alignment from act 5. It's learning syntax, semantics, world facts, programming languages, multiple-step reasoning that arises by accident from imitating humans who reason &mdash; everything that's in the data, at every level of abstraction, all in the same big tower of matrix multiplications and softmaxes.

This is the move that ate the field.

In 2014, attention was a clever fix for a translation problem. In 2017, somebody realised it was strong enough to support a whole architecture by itself. In 2020, scaling that architecture up produced GPT-3, which surprised everyone by being good at things nobody had explicitly trained it to do. In 2022, ChatGPT shipped. In 2026, you and I sit here looking at six little boxes on a screen and reading about the mechanism underneath everything.

The next chapter, *Transformers*, builds the full architecture &mdash; positional encodings, layer norm, feed-forward blocks, residuals, the whole thing. After that, *Language models* shows what happens when you scale it up and train on the internet. After that, *RLHF* shows how you teach the scaled-up model to behave. The whole right half of the constellation grows out of this chapter.

If you came to this site to understand the maths behind AI, you've just crossed the line. You're past the foundations and into the architecture. Attention is the architecture.

Onward.
